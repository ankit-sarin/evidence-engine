SCREENING ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA

INCLUSION CRITERIA:
  - English language
  - Peer-reviewed original research (journal article or full conference paper)
  - Involves a physical surgical task (cadaver, phantom, animal, or human)
  - Must involve an autonomous or semi-autonomous robotic component that CONTROLS or DIRECTS a physical robot to execute a surgical action (not purely teleoperated or manual)
  - Simulation studies included ONLY IF they demonstrate or evaluate autonomous/semi-autonomous robotic behavior in the simulation (not simulation frameworks, physics engines, or virtual environments described without autonomous robot behavior)
  - Published between 2010 and 2025

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
  - Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, or scoping reviews
  - Editorials, commentaries, or letters to the editor
  - Conference abstracts without full paper
  - Non-surgical robotics (industrial, warehouse, rehabilitation, exoskeletons, prosthetics, assistive devices)
  - Purely teleoperated/master-slave robotic surgery where the human directly controls all robot movements in real-time (e.g., standard da Vinci case series, joystick-controlled systems). There must be an autonomous or semi-autonomous component where the system makes independent decisions about motion, planning, or execution.
  - ML/CV methodology papers that only analyze, classify, segment, track, or predict surgical data (video, images, sensor data) without controlling a robot to execute a surgical action (e.g., planning-only papers — surgical motion, path, or task planning computed from data without controlling a robot to execute it)
  - Studies with no autonomous or semi-autonomous component (e.g., hardware, sensor, or instrument papers that describe a robot or device without autonomous control)
  - Papers with no abstract or insufficient information to determine eligibility — do not default to inclusion when evidence is absent

PICO FRAMEWORK:
  Population:   Studies involving autonomous or semi-autonomous surgical robotic systems performing physical surgical tasks on tissue, phantoms, or in-vivo models.
  Intervention: Any level of surgical autonomy (Levels 1–5 per the Yang et al. taxonomy), ranging from robot assistance under continuous human control to fully autonomous task execution.
  Comparator:   Human-controlled (teleoperated) surgical systems, manual surgery, or no comparator.
  Outcomes:     Task accuracy or error rate; Task completion time; Safety events (tissue damage, unplanned stops, collisions); Autonomy level achieved (Yang et al. Level 1–5); Surgeon workload or cognitive load metrics

SPECIALTY SCOPE:
  Included specialties:
    - abdominal surgery
    - gynecologic surgery
    - urologic surgery
    - thoracic surgery
    - general surgery
    - head and neck surgery
    - bench/preclinical autonomy
  Excluded specialties:
    - dental surgery
    - oral and maxillofacial surgery
    - ophthalmic surgery
    - orthopedic surgery
    - dermatologic surgery
    - neurosurgery
  Notes: Papers describing bench/preclinical autonomous tasks (e.g., suturing, knot-tying on phantoms) are included regardless of the specialty context, as these represent generalizable autonomous capabilities. Orthopedic and neurosurgery papers are excluded only when the autonomous task is specialty-specific (e.g., autonomous bone drilling, stereotactic targeting). If the paper describes a generalizable autonomous task performed in an orthopedic or neurosurgical context, include it.
